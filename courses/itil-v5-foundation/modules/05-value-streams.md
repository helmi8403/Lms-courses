# 5. Value streams: improve the whole journey

## Learning goals

Describe an end-to-end value stream, identify delay and rework, and choose measures that reveal whether the consumer's outcome improves.

A value stream represents the connected work that creates value for a particular purpose. A process describes a repeatable set of activities; a value stream combines relevant processes, practices, people and technology around an outcome. One practice can contribute to several streams.

Harbour's “become ready to learn” journey begins before account creation. A learner identifies a course, confirms suitability, obtains approval or payment, receives access, completes orientation and begins meaningful study. Optimizing only account creation may have little effect if enrollment approval consumes five days.

## How to investigate a stream

Start and end with a meaningful demand and outcome. Observe real work rather than describing only the documented ideal. Capture activities, owners, handoffs, queues, decisions, rework and information dependencies. Include supplier boundaries and the learner's own work.

Separate **processing time** from **elapsed time**. Ten minutes of work can sit in a two-day queue. A useful measurement set includes lead time, work in progress, throughput, quality or rework, and outcome indicators. Do not compare unlike cases without considering complexity and demand variation.

A bottleneck limits the flow of the whole stream. Making another activity faster may merely increase the queue before that bottleneck. Repeatedly interrupted staff may spend more time restarting work than the interruption itself takes. Reducing unnecessary handoffs or improving information quality can be more valuable than adding automation.

## Example

Fictional observations show: suitability check 15 minutes, manager approval two days, account creation two minutes, orientation 30 minutes. Harbour automates account creation to 20 seconds. That is a real local improvement, but it barely changes the learner's total wait. A better investigation examines approval needs and how complete information reaches the approver. Necessary controls should be simplified, not silently bypassed.

## Exercise and model reasoning

Map “restore the ability to submit an assessment.” Include detection, learner contact, triage, specialist help, restoration, confirmation and learning. Identify where the learner waits and what information is lost in handoffs.

A strong answer measures time until the learner can continue, not just time until an engineer starts. It distinguishes immediate restoration from follow-up prevention, identifies ownership across teams, and uses anonymized evidence. Improve one significant constraint, then measure the whole stream again.

**Discussion:** A team increases ticket throughput while repeat incidents grow. Explain why this can be declining performance despite an impressive activity metric.
