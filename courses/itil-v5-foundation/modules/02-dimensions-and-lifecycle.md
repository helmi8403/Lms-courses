# 2. Four dimensions and the product/service lifecycle

## Learning goals

Use four dimensions to diagnose an unbalanced design. Explain eight lifecycle activities without treating them as a mandatory one-way sequence.

## 2.1 Four dimensions as a balanced review

**Organizations and people:** Consider skills, responsibilities, structure, culture and communication. Harbour's support team needs both technical access and the authority to escalate exam interruptions. A new process is not usable if no one has time or competence to perform it.

**Information and technology:** Consider data, knowledge, systems, integration, security and the suitability of tools. A course may run correctly while its reporting data is misleading. Define ownership of information, appropriate access and retention, and how evidence reaches decisions.

**Partners and suppliers:** Consider external capabilities, dependencies, contracts and coordination. A video supplier may meet its own technical target while Harbour learners still suffer because the integration fails. Decide how incidents crossing organizational boundaries will be managed.

**Value streams and processes:** Consider the coordinated activities needed to achieve outcomes. A technically correct approval step may create a queue longer than the work itself. Determine handoffs, controls, measures and feedback across the whole stream.

These dimensions interact. Buying automation changes technology, responsibilities, supplier dependence and workflow. External conditions such as regulation, economics, social expectations and technology change can affect each dimension. A balanced review does not mean equal spending on each; it means none is ignored.

## 2.2 Eight lifecycle activities

The lifecycle is iterative. An operational problem may lead back to discovery or design; learning from support can reshape an offering. The sequence below is a teaching route, not a compulsory waterfall.

| Activity | Harbour example | Useful evidence | Typical mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | Understand why shift workers abandon courses | Interviews, usage patterns, unmet needs | Selecting a tool before understanding need |
| Design | Shape the learning journey and service behavior | Experience sketches, requirements, operational assumptions | Designing only the interface |
| Acquire | Obtain required content, capabilities or components | Supplier evaluation, rights, integration constraints | Assuming a purchased component removes accountability |
| Build | Create and integrate the product/service components | Tested increments, traceable decisions | Equating completed code with a usable service |
| Transition | Prepare a change for use and operational support | Readiness evidence, support knowledge, release arrangements | Treating handover as an email sent after launch |
| Operate | Keep the underlying environment functioning | Health, capacity and reliability evidence | Measuring server health instead of user impact |
| Deliver | Make the service available to consumers as intended | Fulfilled access, agreed performance and consumption evidence | Assuming availability guarantees value |
| Support | Help consumers and address disruptions or requests | Resolved cases, usable knowledge, recurring-problem signals | Closing tickets without resolving the user's need |

Operate, deliver and support are closely related but are useful perspectives rather than interchangeable words. Running the platform, providing the learning service, and helping a learner recover access are different responsibilities that must work together.

## 2.3 Make decisions with lifecycle feedback

Suppose learners report repeated failed video uploads. Support restores immediate access; operate investigates resource constraints; discovery asks whether upload behavior reflects an unmet need; design may introduce resumable uploads; acquire may evaluate an external component. Improvement is distributed across activities.

A decision to retire a feature also needs coordination: understand affected users, communicate the alternative, migrate necessary information with permission, and ensure support can answer questions. Never assume retirement is only deletion of a software component.

## Exercise

Harbour purchases an AI tutor but has no escalation process when its advice is wrong. Describe one gap in each dimension and identify lifecycle activities that should respond.

**Model reasoning:** People need accountable reviewers; information and technology need quality controls and data handling; suppliers need clarity on support and model limitations; value streams need a route from feedback to correction. Discovery and design revisit the need and safeguards, acquisition examines provider responsibilities, and support captures incidents. The answer should not be “buy a better model” without the other dimensions.

## Deliverable

Write a one-page balanced-review note: intended outcome, four-dimension findings, lifecycle activities affected, decision owner, evidence required and remaining uncertainty. State assumptions explicitly.
